THE WORKING

EEG exploits dipole. Dipole essentially is the two different chargers that are separated by a distance. So using electrodes we can generate a readout of the difference in the charges. How does our brain has areas with different charges. 
For eg. A neuron which is resting has more negative charge than outside (about -70mV) 

Neurons communicate via a space called synapse using neurotransmitters. Once neurotransmitters reach the synapse, they reach the next neuron where they being to receptors where 2 things can happen :

1) Excitatory Post Synaptic Potential - Neurotransmitters causes +ve charges to flow into the neuron making inside of neuron more +ve. This obviously makes the space outside more -ve relatively. Now since one outside part is more -ve the other end becomes +ve relatively. We have a dipole essentially.

2) Inhibitry Post Synaptic Potential is essentially the opposite of what happens (the charges accumulated).

So these 2 creates dipoles at the opposite ends of the neurons. But dipole for neuron is extremely small. But in celebral cortex just below scalp there are billions of neurons parallel to each other and perpendicular to scalp. Clusters of these neurons give individual neurons gives dipoles. So we can stick in 2 electrodes and measure the difference which is ESSENTIALLY AN EEG SIGNAL IS.

THE GRAPHS

Standard EEG instrument contains like 32 electrodes but here we are using only 14. Signals are measured via 2 methods - 
1) Common reference montage where we take a reference electrode and measure every other from it.

2) Compare every electrode to electrode next to it known as Bipolar Montage.

EEG Patterns are commonly described in terms of the signal (there are 5 but we are using 4 except theta) as follows:

1) Sleep / Delta - < 4 Hz
2) Relaxing / Theta - 4 - 8 Hz
3) Attention / Alpha - 8 - 13 Hz
4) Active mind / Beta - 13 - 35 Hz
5) High Concetration / Gamma - > 35 Hz

NOTE:Interictal epileptiform discharges (IEDs) are abnormal brain wave patterns seen on an electroencephalogram (EEG) that occur between actual clinical seizures. They serve as a primary diagnostic hallmark of epilepsy, indicating underlying hyperirritable brain tissue without causing a full seizure episode at that moment.

What does 128 Hz look like mathematically?

The time between samples is: 1/128 = 0.0078125 seconds or 7.8125 milliseconds.

So approximately every 7.8 milliseconds, another EEG value is recorded. REMEMBER THIS IS NOT THE OSCILLATIONS WHICH EXIST IN THE SIGNAL.

WHAT IS SPECTRAL ANALYSIS? (ref:https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DoSLMEEo1Y0)
The raw EEG waveform is the mixture of all of them.

So instead of only asking: "What voltage did we measure at this moment?"

we can ask: "How much activity exists at different frequencies?"

That is the idea behind spectral analysis.

Power is basically how strong a particular frequency component is in the EEG signal. Since 10 Hz falls roughly in the alpha range, we would say: alpha-band power is relatively high.

What is a PSD ?
PSD is Power Spectral Density. PSD is basically taking variations of a signal and taking its Fourier Transform and averaging it out all together.
PSD tells us how the signal's power is distributed across frequency. It gives us a Power vs Frequency.

What is 16-bit A/D resolution ?
The electrical EEG signal coming from the electrodes is an analog signal. A 16-bit converter can theoretically represent: 2^16
different digital levels.

That is: 65,536 possible levels.

You do not need to know more than that for this paper. The key idea is: the EEG voltage is analog, but the computer stores digitized numerical samples.

So acquisition involves two kinds of discretization:

TIME
continuous signal
↓
sample at 128 times/sec
AMPLITUDE
continuous voltage
↓
convert into digital values using 16-bit resolution

That distinction is useful. Sampling frequency concerns the time axis. Bit resolution concerns the amplitude axis.

DATA PREPROCESSING
Raw EEG is not clean.So conceptually: Raw EEG = brain-related signal + movement artifacts + electrical noise + other unwanted components

The goal is not to magically make the signal perfect. The goal is to reduce unwanted contamination so later analysis is more reliable. 4 steps are followed.


>>>>>STEP 1 : High pass filter the raw data at 1Hz.
What is High Pass filter (ref : https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RAhcsDwp7iA). Allows all frequency above the cut off frequency (1Hz) and 
attenuates (lower the amplitude) the frequency below it.

So Very slow components < ~1 Hz -> reduced
Higher-frequency EEG activity -> retained


>>>>>STEP 2 : Remove line noise.
Line noise comes from the electrical power environment. Its nothing but electrical interference.

>>>> STEP 3 : Artifact Subspace Reconstruction (ASR) (ref: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wpq8S7PKjp4)

This is the step the authors emphasize as especially important.
Think of an EEG signal like this:

normal EEG:
~~~~~~/\~~~~~/\~~~~~~

Then suddenly the subject moves or some large artifact appears:

~~~~~~/\~~~~~~/^^^^^^^^^\~~~~~~

That huge abnormal burst can dominate the signal.

ASR tries to identify these abnormal large-amplitude components and reconstruct the affected data.

>>>>>> STEP 4 : EEG is measured as a voltage difference, so we always need some reference. Instead of comparing 
every electrode to one fixed reference, average referencing uses the average signal across electrodes as the reference.

Then for a channel like F3: F3_new​ = F3 − V_avg​ where V_avg is essentially the average of all voltages.

So the whole preprocessing pipeline is
RAW EEG
   ↓
High-pass at 1 Hz
   ↓
Remove line noise
   ↓
ASR
   ↓
Average re-reference
   ↓
PREPROCESSED EEG

Sliding window & Shift

So imagine just one EEG channel, say F3.You already know the EEG sampling frequency is: 128 Hz which means: 128 measurements are recorded every second for each channel.

After 2 seconds = 256 samples
After 4 seconds = 512 samples ---> This is our window size or 4 seconds of EEG. 

So for a 2.5 min, instead of doing all 150s, they take a smaller chunk of 4 second or 512 samples

Now the shift = 128 samples
Since 128 samples = 1 second, instead of jumping ahead by the full 4 seconds, they move the window forward by only 1 second.

So window 1 has 0 - 4 second and 1 - 5 second with shared portion as 1 - 4 second and thus a overlap of 3 / 4 = 75%.

Why use windows?

Imagine calculating one number from the entire 2.5 minutes.

You'd lose a lot of information about how EEG changes throughout the recording.

Instead:

2.5-minute EEG
       ↓
many short 4-second segments
       ↓
extract spectral features from each segment

This gives many local observations rather than one giant summary.

At a conceptual level, each segment can be represented by:

56 PSD features

COHEN's kappa
Cohen's kappa

The paper also reports: kappa = 0.46

with an expected random-chance accuracy of: 42.4%

This needs explaining because accuracy alone doesn't tell the whole story.
Imagine the classes are not perfectly balanced.
Suppose one class appears much more often than others. A dumb model could predict that common class repeatedly and still achieve non-trivial accuracy.
Cohen's kappa tries to account for the amount of agreement we would expect by chance.

Conceptually:

κ= maximum (agreement − chance agreement) / (observed agreement − chance agreement)
	​
The basic interpretation is:

Accuracy
→ How often were predictions correct?

Kappa
→ How much better was agreement than what we would expect from chance?

It means:

Here the 69% means that after the SVR predictions were converted into Low/Moderate/High classes, 
69% of the evaluated test classifications matched the true classes.
